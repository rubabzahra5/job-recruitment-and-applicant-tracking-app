# TalentBridge Scam-Job Policy (Draft)

**Status:** Draft for review by a qualified lawyer. Not published as final.
**Public version:** `app/(public)/scam-policy/page.tsx` (`/scam-policy`)
presents this policy to candidates and employers and must be kept in step
with this document.
**Last updated (draft):** 24 November 2026

TalentBridge is free for candidates — always. Any "job" that asks a
candidate for money is a scam, and this policy sets out the rules, what
happens when they are broken, and how candidates can protect themselves.

## 1. The rules for employers

1. **Never ask candidates for money.** An employer, recruiter, or anyone
   claiming to represent an employer must never ask a candidate for:
   - application, processing, or "registration" fees;
   - security deposits or refundable deposits of any kind;
   - payment for training, training materials, or "starter kits" as a
     condition of being hired;
   - gift cards, mobile top-ups, or cryptocurrency;
   - bank account details, card numbers, PINs, or one-time passcodes
     (OTPs) during the application or interview process.
   There are no exceptions. TalentBridge charges candidates nothing, and
   legitimate employers on TalentBridge do not either.
2. **Jobs must be real.** Every posting must be a genuine, current
   vacancy at the named organisation, with a truthful title, description,
   location, and salary information (PKR). Posting a vacancy that does
   not exist, or harvesting CVs for unrelated purposes, is prohibited.
3. **Post as who you are.** Postings must come from the organisation's
   own verified account. Impersonating another company, a government
   body, or TalentBridge itself is prohibited.
4. **Keep hiring on the platform.** Directing candidates to continue the
   "application" through unofficial channels in order to extract payment
   or personal documents (for example, demanding a CNIC copy or bank
   details before any offer, via personal messaging accounts) is treated
   as a scam indicator. [VERIFY] The point at which requesting identity
   documents becomes legitimate in a Pakistan hiring process (e.g. after
   a written offer) should be confirmed so this rule can be stated
   precisely. See `docs/open-questions.md`.
5. **No misleading pay or conditions.** Salary ranges, job type, and
   workplace (on-site/remote) must match the real offer.

## 2. Consequences ladder

Breaches are handled in proportion to severity and history. Money-asking
and impersonation are severe breaches and may skip earlier steps.

| Step | Action | Typical trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Warning** to the organisation, with the rule cited | First, minor breach (e.g. misleading job details) |
| 2 | **Job removed** (the posting is taken down) | Misleading or non-existent vacancy; repeat minor breach |
| 3 | **Organisation suspended** (cannot post or respond until reviewed) | Money-asking, impersonation, or repeated breaches |
| 4 | **Account terminated** (organisation and responsible user accounts closed) | Confirmed scam, fraud, or breach after suspension |

Decisions are recorded in the platform audit log. An organisation may
respond to any action via the contact page and ask for a review; the
appeal route for "No response" marks is described in the appeal process
document, and the same contact channel is used for scam-action reviews.

## 3. How reports are handled

Anyone — candidate, employer, or visitor — can report a suspected scam
via the report-a-scam page (`/report-scam`), without needing an account.

- Reports go to the platform's trust & safety queue (visible to super
  administrators in the control center).
- **Acknowledgement:** we aim to acknowledge reports within **2 business
  days** (Monday–Friday, excluding Pakistan public holidays).
- **Initial review:** we aim to complete an initial review within **5
  business days**. Urgent reports (money already sent, impersonation of a
  real company) are prioritised.
- **Outcomes:** the job may be removed, the organisation warned,
  suspended, or terminated per §2. Reporters who gave an email address are
  told the outcome in general terms (we cannot share another account's
  private data).
- Reports made in bad faith (for example, to harm a competitor) are
  themselves a breach of the terms of use.

## 4. Candidate guidance — red flags

Stop and report it if a "job" involves any of these:

- **Any request for money** — fees, deposits, "training charges",
  equipment purchases, or gift cards, at any stage.
- **Requests for bank details, card PINs, or OTP codes** before you have
  a written offer and a verified employer.
- **Pay that sounds too good** for the work described, with vague duties.
- **Pressure to move off the platform** quickly to personal WhatsApp or
  Telegram accounts, especially before any interview.
- **Interviews only by text chat**, with no real person, no office, and
  no verifiable company presence.
- **A company name that almost matches** a famous company, or an email
  address from a free mailbox claiming to be a large employer.
- **Being asked to receive or forward money or parcels** — this can
  involve you in fraud.
- **Vague job, instant "selection"** without any interview or assessment.

If you have already sent money: contact your bank or payment provider
immediately, keep all messages and receipts, and still report it to us —
your report protects the next candidate.
