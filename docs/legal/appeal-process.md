# "No Response" Appeal Process (Draft)

**Status:** Draft for review by a qualified lawyer. Not published as final.
**Public cross-reference:** the scam-policy page (`/scam-policy`) carries a
short employer-facing summary of this process; the full text lives here.
**Last updated (draft):** 24 November 2026

## 1. What a "No response" mark means

TalentBridge's response rule expects an employer to give a **first
response** to every application within **7 business days** (Monday–Friday,
excluding Pakistan public holidays, Asia/Karachi). A reminder point is
shown at day 5.

- A "first response" is the employer moving the application out of the
  initial stage (for example to screening, shortlisted, interview, offer,
  hired, or rejected) on the platform.
- If no first response is recorded by the deadline, the application is
  shown to the candidate as **"No response"**, and it counts against the
  organisation's **public response rate**.
- The mark is derived automatically from application timestamps and stage
  history. It is a factual statement about what happened **on the
  platform** — it is not an accusation of bad faith, and it can be wrong
  in the situations listed in §2.

## 2. Grounds for appeal

An organisation may appeal a "No response" mark (or its effect on the
response rate) when:

1. **Responded outside the platform** — the candidate was genuinely
   contacted or progressed (email, phone, interview held) but the
   platform stage was not updated; evidence required (§4).
2. **Candidate withdrew** — the candidate withdrew or told the employer
   they were no longer interested before the deadline.
3. **Role filled and communicated** — the position was filled or closed
   and applicants were informed, but the applications were not closed on
   the platform.
4. **Platform error** — a technical fault (including a declared
   internet-disruption period, per the platform's Pakistan-first
   commitments) prevented a timely response.
5. **Public-holiday dispute** — the employer believes the deadline was
   calculated over a Pakistan public holiday that the platform's holiday
   calendar missed or mis-dated (Islamic holidays are moon-dependent and
   can shift by a day).

Disagreement with the rule itself is not a ground for appeal, but
feedback about the rule is welcome via the same channel.

## 3. How to appeal (the channel that exists today)

Appeals are handled through the platform's existing contact channel —
there is no separate appeals form:

1. Go to the **contact page (`/contact`)**. You do not need to be signed
   in, but appeal from the email address associated with your employer
   account so we can verify you represent the organisation.
2. Use the subject line: **`No response appeal — <organisation name>`**.
3. In the message, include:
   - the organisation name and the job title;
   - the candidate's name (or application date) for each application
     being appealed;
   - the ground for appeal (from §2);
   - a short explanation and your evidence (§4).
4. One message can cover several applications for the same job.

Employers can reach the same channel from the response-rule alert on
their dashboard, which links to the summary of this process on the
scam-policy page.

## 4. Evidence to attach or describe

- **Off-platform response:** the email (headers/date), call log entry, or
  interview invitation showing the candidate was contacted before the
  deadline. Redact anything unrelated.
- **Withdrawal:** the candidate's message or a note of when/how they
  withdrew.
- **Role filled and communicated:** the closure announcement or message
  sent to applicants, with its date.
- **Platform error:** dates, what happened, and any error messages seen.
- **Holiday dispute:** the holiday and date in question.

Evidence is handled under the privacy policy and is only used to decide
the appeal.

## 5. Review timelines (policy commitments)

Business days are Monday–Friday, excluding Pakistan public holidays
(Asia/Karachi) — the same calendar the response rule itself uses.

- **Acknowledgement:** within **2 business days** of the appeal being
  received.
- **Decision:** within **5 business days** of the appeal being received
  (if we need more information, we will say so within that period and the
  clock restarts when it is provided).
- The decision is sent to the email address the appeal came from.

## 6. Outcomes

- **Mark removed** — the appeal succeeds in full: the "No response" mark
  is removed for the application(s) and the organisation's response rate
  is recalculated without it.
- **Response rate adjusted** — the appeal succeeds in part (for example,
  some applications in a batch): the rate is adjusted for the applications
  where the mark was wrong.
- **Upheld** — the mark stands, with a short explanation of why.
- Where the appeal reveals a platform error (including a holiday-calendar
  error), the underlying data or calendar is fixed so the same mistake
  does not recur, and affected organisations are corrected in bulk where
  identifiable.

## 7. Abuse of appeals

- Appeals must be truthful. Fabricated evidence (for example, a doctored
  email) is a serious breach: the mark is reinstated, the response rate is
  adjusted back, and the organisation may be warned, suspended, or
  terminated under the consequences ladder in the scam-job policy.
- Repeated speculative appeals for the same application after a decision
  will not be re-reviewed unless new evidence is provided.
- Candidates are never penalised because an employer appealed.
